Security & privacy

Is Greenroom watching your screen? No.

Greenroom is not AI, and it doesn't watch what you do. It's an ordinary Mac app that runs a fixed checklist — open a few apps, line up their windows, start your meeting — the same way you would by hand, then gets out of the way. Here is exactly how, in plain language, with the full technical detail below for anyone who wants to verify it.

The short version

Think of it as a stagehand, not a robot

Before a play, a stagehand sets everything up — moves the furniture, aims the lights, opens the curtain — then steps offstage. They follow the same script every night and make no decisions of their own.

That's Greenroom. When you press Start, it performs a fixed sequence of ordinary actions — launch OBS, place your windows, start or join your Zoom meeting — and then it's done. There is no AI in the app. It doesn't learn, doesn't analyse what's on your screen, and doesn't decide anything for itself. (AI helped write the code, the same way a developer would; it is not running inside the app.)

And because the whole app is open source, none of this is something you have to take on faith — every line is public and checkable.

Setting the record straight

What Greenroom never does

The scary version of "an app that arranges your screen" would do these things. Greenroom does none of them — and the source code proves it.

✕

No AI or "intelligence"

No machine-learning models, no cloud AI, no decision-making. It runs a fixed script.

✕

Doesn't read your screen

Greenroom captures zero pixels. Your screen is captured by OBS — a separate app you can see running — only while you're presenting.

✕

Not a keylogger

It registers four specific keyboard shortcuts with macOS and is told only when those exact combos are pressed. It cannot see anything else you type.

✕

Doesn't read your windows or browsing

When it lines up a window it only reads its title, size and position — never its contents, your tabs, or your history.

✕

No analytics or tracking

No telemetry, no usage tracking, no crash-phone-home. Greenroom has no server of its own to send anything to.

✕

Nothing hidden in the background

It acts only when you press Start, a shortcut, or a button — never silently on its own (aside from a routine check for app updates).

What actually happens

When you press Start

Three ordinary steps. Each uses a specific macOS permission that you grant once and can revoke anytime.

It sets up your camera view

Greenroom launches OBS (a well-known, separate open-source app) and tells it to build the "your screen with you in the corner" layout. OBS does the actual screen and webcam capture — Greenroom just configures it over a local connection.

Camera · Screen Recording (OBS's)

It arranges your windows

It opens your chosen app and the meeting, then moves those specific windows into a tidy layout using the same macOS window controls any window-manager uses. It positions named windows only — it never touches their contents.

Accessibility · Automation

It starts your Zoom meeting

It creates or joins your meeting through Zoom's own official software — exactly what happens when you use Zoom normally. By default it joins muted and with your camera off.

Microphone (muted by default)

Architecture

How the pieces fit together

Greenroom is an orchestrator: it drives OBS and macOS, and speaks to Zoom and GitHub. Everything inside the box below runs on your Mac — only two links ever cross to the internet, and both are ones you'd expect.

The Start sequence, step by step

  1. Launch & connect to OBS

    Greenroom starts OBS hidden (or reuses a warm instance) and connects to it over ws://127.0.0.1:4455 with a local password — an authenticated loopback socket, never exposed off the machine.

  2. Build the scene

    Over that socket it configures the OBS scene: a screen-capture source, your webcam keyed into a bubble (or cutout/presenter), and it starts the OBS Virtual Camera. OBS performs all capture; Greenroom only sends configuration commands.

  3. Create or join the meeting

    In parallel it talks to Zoom over HTTPS — the Meeting SDK and REST API — to create a fresh meeting (as host) or join your existing one, with the OBS Virtual Camera as the video source, muted and camera-off by default.

  4. Position the windows

    It opens your chosen app and moves it, the Zoom window, and the chat into their slots using the Accessibility API (AXUIElement) and Apple Events for browsers — reading only each window's title, size and position, never its contents.

  5. Hand off to you

    Greenroom drops to the background and the session is live. From here it's idle except for the periodic signed update check. Pressing End Session tears it all down in order.

Permissions

What it asks macOS for — and why each is narrow

macOS forces you to approve each of these yourself, and you can turn any of them off at any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security. Greenroom can't grant itself anything.

Camera & Microphone
So your webcam and voice can reach the meeting. Greenroom itself doesn't open a camera session — OBS and Zoom's software do; Greenroom only picks which device to use. It joins meetings muted and camera-off by default.
Accessibility System-wide window control
The standard macOS way to move another app's windows. Greenroom uses it only to read a window's title/size/position and to move specific, named windows (your main app; the "Zoom Meeting" window) into the layout. It reads no window contents and no screen pixels. This permission is broad by design — the honest safeguard is that the code only ever targets those named windows, which you can verify in the source.
Automation Apple Events
Lets Greenroom ask your browser to open a fresh window at the right size and address. It's limited to Chromium-family browsers (Chrome, Edge, Brave, Ulaa…) and does only that — it can't read your history or tabs. macOS asks per-app the first time.
Screen Recording Belongs to OBS
Greenroom does not request screen recording. OBS holds that permission and performs the capture itself; Greenroom only hands OBS the identifier of the display to show.
Notifications
Optional. Used only for local banners like "Recording started / saved." Nothing is sent anywhere.

Your data

Where everything goes

Greenroom talks to only two outside parties, and both are ones you'd expect.

Zoom Leaves your Mac

Your meeting audio, video and chat, and requests to create or join your own meetings — through Zoom's official software. This is identical to using Zoom directly.

GitHub Leaves your Mac

A periodic check for app updates, and the download if you accept one. Updates are cryptographically signed, so only genuine Greenroom releases can install.

OBS Stays local

Greenroom controls OBS over your Mac's internal loopback connection (127.0.0.1). This never touches the network. Recordings are saved as files in your Documents folder.

That's the entire list. No analytics, no tracking, no Greenroom server — because there isn't one.

Every network endpoint

api.zoom.us / zoom.us — Zoom's official API & meeting servers (your meetings).  ·  github.com + sibhimanyu.github.io — app-update check & download.  ·  ws://127.0.0.1:4455 — local OBS control, never leaves the machine. Nothing else. Two entitlements only: camera and microphone. No sandbox-escape or screen-recording entitlement (screen recording is OBS's).

Frameworks & updates

Built on Apple's own frameworks only — AppKit, SwiftUI, AVFoundation (device enumeration + recording playback), ApplicationServices (Accessibility), Carbon (the four global hotkeys), CryptoKit (SHA-256/HMAC for local auth) — plus the Zoom Meeting SDK and Sparkle for updates. No CoreML, Vision, or any ML/inference framework is linked. Updates are delivered as EdDSA-signed archives: the app verifies each download against a public key baked into the binary, so only releases signed with the developer's private key can install.

Full honesty

What isn't perfect yet

A trustworthy explanation names the weak spots too. Both of these are local-only — nothing here is exposed over the network.

Zoom credentials are stored in plain text on your Mac

The Zoom app credentials you enter are saved in Greenroom's local preferences as plain text — not encrypted. (Greenroom doesn't use the macOS Keychain at all.) They never leave your Mac except to Zoom itself. The practical risk is purely local: someone who already has access to your Mac user account could read them.

Worth keeping in perspective: these are app-level Zoom API keys scoped to running meetings under your account — not your Zoom login password — and you can revoke them anytime in the Zoom Marketplace.

Separately, the optional "export settings" file (used to set up a colleague's Mac) also contains these credentials in plaintext by design — hand it over directly and delete it after importing.
