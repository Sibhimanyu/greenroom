Privacy policy

What Greenroom keeps, and where it goes.

Greenroom runs on your Mac. Your recordings and transcripts stay there. This page lists the few things that do leave, and why.

Effective 2026-11-20. Greenroom is free and open source: the code is on GitHub, so you can check any claim here. For a diagram of every network call, see Privacy & safety.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Greenroom app for macOS and the small signing service it uses when you sign in with Zoom. Greenroom is published by Sibhimanyu G, an individual developer. Contact: sibhi.gv@gmail.com.

The short version

  • Recordings, transcripts, notes and clips are written to a folder on your Mac. Greenroom does not upload them anywhere unless you turn on YouTube upload or your own agent.
  • Greenroom has no accounts and no database of its own. We hold no profile of you.
  • When you sign in with Zoom, one small server checks that you are a real Zoom user. It keeps nothing.
  • Anonymous usage counts go to Zoho Apptics. You can switch them off.
  • We do not sell data. We do not show ads.

What goes to Zoom

Greenroom uses Zoom's sign-in (OAuth) in your normal browser. You approve the permissions on Zoom's own page. Greenroom never sees your Zoom password. It asks only for what it needs:

  • user:read:user: read your name, email and IDs, to show who is signed in.
  • user:read:token and user:read:zak: get your host key, so Greenroom can start your meeting as you.
  • meeting:read:list_meetings and meeting:read:meeting: list your meetings and read their details, so you can pick one.
  • meeting:write:meeting and meeting:update:meeting: create a meeting and edit one you made in Greenroom.
  • meeting:update:status: end a meeting you are hosting, when you press End.
  • meeting:delete:meeting: delete a scheduled meeting, only when you choose to.

The meeting itself runs through Zoom's Meeting SDK. Zoom's own privacy statement covers what Zoom does with meeting data.

What goes to the signing server

Zoom's Meeting SDK needs a signed pass before it starts. The signing key cannot be placed inside a public app, so a small service signs the pass for you. It runs as a Zoho Catalyst function, in Zoho's India data centre.

  • Greenroom sends your Zoom access token, and sometimes a meeting number, to that service over HTTPS (TLS 1.2 or 1.3).
  • The service uses the token for one thing: asking Zoom who you are, to check you are a real Zoom user. If you are starting a meeting, it also asks Zoom whether that meeting is yours.
  • It then returns the signed pass. The code stores no token, no profile and no meeting number, and writes no log lines of its own.
  • Zoho Catalyst keeps platform logs of function runs: seven days in its development environment and fourteen in production, according to Zoho's documentation. Zoho does not say which request details those logs hold. The code never puts your token or profile in them.

We never see your Zoom password, and the service never gets your recordings.

Anonymous usage counts (Zoho Apptics)

Greenroom reports usage to Zoho Apptics. This is on by default. The report is made of counts, rough time bands and fixed codes. For example: a meeting ran 30 to 60 minutes, a clip was taken, a setting was switched on, a feature failed.

Apptics also registers a random device ID for the install, which is not tied to your name or Zoom account. It never includes student or participant names, meeting IDs, file paths, web addresses, chat text, transcript text or search phrases. The status log is deliberately not sent. If Greenroom crashes, a crash report is sent as well.

To turn it off: Settings, Layout, Privacy. This stops every call, crash reports included. Apptics keeps event data for about 30 days.

Reports you send (Heresay)

Greenroom has a Report button. If you press it and send a report, the text you wrote is sent to the Heresay service, and the maintainer reads it. Write only what you are happy to share.

The report also carries technical details Greenroom adds: app version, macOS version, device model, language, time zone, light or dark theme, network type, window and screen size, the screen you were on, and how long the app had been running. You can leave "Attach what happened" on or off; when on, it adds a short trail of screens and buttons you clicked (never what you typed) and the app's own error messages. No screenshots or recordings are attached. Crashes are not sent automatically: one waits on your Mac and goes with a report only if you send one. Greenroom does not tell Heresay who you are, and you can type a name or email in the report if you want a reply.

YouTube uploads

Off by default. If you connect a Google account and choose "ask" or "upload automatically", a finished recording is uploaded over HTTPS to your own channel, unlisted or private as you chose. The recording shows your shared screen and has your voice, and it may show or include other people in the class. Greenroom keeps a refresh token locally, on your Mac, in its app settings. Disconnect revokes it at Google.

Cues lookups

Off by default. When Cues is on and someone names a book, a topic or a video, Greenroom sends only the short search phrase (a few words, such as a title) to Google Books, Open Library, Wikipedia, Wikiquote or YouTube. It never sends audio or the transcript. A phrase that matches a name in the meeting is dropped first, except inside a quotation. The phrase and the service it went to are written to a local log on your Mac.

Things that stay on your Mac

  • Speech to text for Cues and Analyse runs on the Mac, with Apple's speech model pinned to on-device or whisper.cpp.
  • Counts such as pace, pauses and facing the room are worked out on the Mac.
  • Class summaries use Apple's on-device model on macOS 26.
  • Analyse can hand a session to an agent you bring yourself (for example Claude Code). It is off by default. If you turn it on, that agent sends data to its own service, under its own terms, and Greenroom cannot describe what it does. It is not given the audio or video.

Who else sees data

Zoom, Zoho (Apptics and the Catalyst hosting), Heresay, and Google or the lookup sites, only as described above and only for the features you use. Update checks go to GitHub. We do not sell or rent your data, and we do not use it for ads.

How long we keep it

  • On your Mac: until you delete it. Greenroom never deletes your session folders on its own.
  • Signing server: nothing is kept by the code. See the note above about the host's platform logs.
  • Apptics: about 30 days.
  • Heresay reports: kept in the maintainer's own Heresay account until the maintainer deletes them.

Deleting your data

  • Sign out of Zoom: Settings, Zoom, Sign out. Greenroom asks Zoom to revoke the sign-in and removes your profile and the Keychain token from your Mac. If Zoom cannot be reached, it still removes them locally, and you can remove the app yourself in Zoom: Apps, Manage, Added Apps.
  • Your recordings and transcripts: delete the session folder in ~/Documents/Greenroom, or delete it from Sessions in the app.
  • YouTube: Settings, YouTube, Disconnect. Videos already uploaded stay on your channel until you delete them there.
  • Usage counts: switch them off in Settings. To ask us about data already sent, write to sibhi.gv@gmail.com.
  • Everything: delete the app and its settings. The settings are in ~/Library/Preferences and ~/Library/Application Support/Greenroom.

Security

Network calls use HTTPS. The signing service talks to Zoom over HTTPS and holds its secret as a hosting secret, not in the code. Updates are signed and checked against a key inside the app. Your Zoom sign-in token is stored in the macOS Keychain. Other local data, including the YouTube token if you connect one, sits in the app's settings on your Mac and is not encrypted by Greenroom, so keep your Mac account protected (FileVault and a login password). The signing service accepts TLS 1.2 and 1.3.

Found a security problem? Write to sibhi.gv@gmail.com or use the Report button.

Children and schools

Greenroom is a tool for teachers and presenters. It is not made for children, and it has no accounts for children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. The people who sign in are adults.

Teachers do use it in classes, and a recording can include students' faces, voices and names. That recording stays on the teacher's Mac, and Greenroom does not send it to us. The school or teacher decides who has consent to record and who may see recordings, and has to follow the school's rules and the laws that apply, such as FERPA in the United States, and COPPA where students are under 13. If a teacher turns on YouTube upload or their own agent, they are choosing to send class material to those services.

Anonymous usage counts carry no names, and the status log with student names is never sent. If you think a child's information reached us, write to sibhi.gv@gmail.com and we will delete it.

Changes

If we change this policy, we change the date at the top and note it in the release notes. The history is public in the GitHub repository.

Your data and your rights

You can see, correct and delete what Greenroom keeps about you.

  • Access and correction. Your recordings, transcripts, notes and settings are on your Mac, where you can open and change them. Settings, Zoom shows the account you are signed in with. Your Zoom profile and meetings are held by Zoom, and you manage them in your Zoom account.
  • Deletion and withdrawing consent. Sign out in Settings, Zoom to revoke Greenroom's access at Zoom and remove the saved sign-in from your Mac. The steps above cover the rest, including removing Greenroom from your Zoom account.
  • Data held by others. Usage counts (Apptics), reports you send (Heresay) and the host's platform logs for the signing service are not on your Mac. To ask about or delete them, write to the contact below.
  • Other rights. If data-protection law where you live gives you more, such as the right to object or to a copy of your data, write to the contact below. We answer within 30 days.
