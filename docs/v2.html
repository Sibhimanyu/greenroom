What one press does

Five things, in order, in about six seconds.

Nothing here is hard on its own. All of it, every single morning, is the problem.

01

OBS starts hidden

A virtual camera publishes the whole screen with you keyed into a corner bubble — or cut out of your background entirely. Students see the book and you.

02

The meeting opens itself

A fresh Zoom meeting is created under your own account and hosted from Greenroom's built-in client. No second app to click through. Joining an existing meeting by link works the same way.

03

Windows take their places

Your reading doc fills the main pane at ½, ⅔ or ¾ of the screen; the meeting tile and chat stack into what's left. Dragged something out of place mid-class? ⌥⌘S snaps it all back.

04

Chat sits beside the book

A standalone chat window in its own tile, so Zoom's panel never slides over the page you're reading from.

05

Recording, if you want it

One press records exactly what the class saw — the composited screen with you in the corner — straight to a file for whoever missed the morning.

—

Set it up once, hand it on

Every credential and preference exports as a single file. A colleague imports it and their Mac is ready. Nobody else touches the Zoom Marketplace.

Setup

On a Mac, in three steps. Once.

Step 01

Download & drag in

Grab the zip from the latest release, unzip, and drag Greenroom.app into Applications.

Step 02

Open Anyway, once

The app isn't notarized, so macOS balks the first time. System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. On macOS 14 and earlier, right-click → Open.

Step 03

Import & go

Import the settings file from whoever set it up, install the free OBS Studio when the built-in guide asks, and grant each permission as it appears.

Is it safe

Everything stays on your Mac, except the one thing that can't.

Greenroom composites video locally and drives your windows through macOS's own Accessibility API. The only traffic that leaves is the Zoom call itself — which was always going to Zoom.
